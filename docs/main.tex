\title{CapDrift-FL: Capability- and Drift-Aware Federated Learning for Heterogeneous Edge AI Systems}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{William Ileka and Brendan Kechtban}
\IEEEauthorblockA{School of Computing, DePaul University, Chicago, IL, USA\\
wileka@depaul.edu; bkechtba@depaul.edu}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Synchronous federated learning must balance the work assigned to clients with different processing speeds and data distributions. CapDrift-FL estimates each client's throughput, assigns a minimum workload, checks update direction before allowing extra computation, and bounds contributions before aggregation. In 25-round simulations across five training seeds under matched deadlines ($T_{\mathrm{target}}=6.5$\,s), CapDrift-FL achieves $94.12\pm1.43\%$ validation accuracy while reducing cumulative straggler synchronization idle waste by $41.4\%$ (from 522.15\,s to 306.06\,s) and elevating hardware compute utilization from $36.5\%$ to $62.8\%$. In clinical cohort volume scaling ($M \in \{763\text{--}5216\}$ images), uniform FedAvg collapses from $92.4\%$ to $76.3\%$ due to local step starvation, while CapDrift-FL maintains $94.03\%$ ($+17.7\%$ margin) and reaches $95.10\pm0.24\%$ on the full cohort. Under extreme pathological Dirichlet skew ($\alpha \le 0.10$), unconstrained FedNova suffers a $93.3\%$ failure rate from nonfinite loss divergence, whereas CapDrift-FL maintains $100\%$ numerical safety and outperforms FedAvg by $+22.75\%$ at $\alpha=0.05$. In containerized physical deployment across Cisco UCS servers, the controller reduced cumulative barrier waiting by $69.1\%$ in Round 2 while achieving $77.78\%$ held-out accuracy. These experiments isolate the exact operating regimes where capability-aware workload pacing and drift gating outperform uniform allocation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Federated learning (FL) enables distributed clients to collaboratively train machine-learning models without transferring their raw datasets to a central coordinator \cite{mcmahan2017}. This approach is particularly relevant to edge artificial intelligence (AI) systems, where data and computational resources are distributed across heterogeneous devices. In synchronous FL, participating clients perform local optimization before their updates are aggregated to construct a global model. Differences in processing capabilities and communication conditions can introduce substantial variations in local training durations. Consequently, faster clients may remain idle while waiting for slower participants, increasing synchronization delays and limiting training efficiency \cite{chai2020tifl,lai2021oort}.

Uniform workload allocation assigns identical local training requirements to participating clients, regardless of their computational capabilities. Although this approach simplifies training coordination, it may underutilize the resources available at faster devices. Capability-aware workload allocation addresses this limitation by adapting local computation to client processing capabilities \cite{li2024fedcompass,narmadha2025fedeff}. Nevertheless, unequal workloads introduce additional optimization challenges when client datasets are not independently and identically distributed (non-IID). Under such conditions, local model updates may differ considerably in direction and magnitude. Increasing local training can amplify these differences, potentially affecting convergence and global model accuracy \cite{li2020fedprox,karimireddy2020scaffold}. Furthermore, excessive local computation may extend synchronization intervals, reducing the expected benefits of workload adaptation.

Existing FL approaches have investigated resource-aware client selection, heterogeneous workload allocation, and optimization mechanisms for non-IID data \cite{lai2021oort,shin2022fedbalancer,wang2020fednova}. Nevertheless, allocating additional computation according to client capability does not necessarily improve learning performance. In particular, adaptive training must account for directional disagreement among local updates and differences in their contributions to global aggregation. These considerations motivate a coordinated approach that regulates local computation while accounting for its effects on model accuracy and execution time.

This paper presents CapDrift-FL, a capability- and drift-aware federated learning framework for heterogeneous edge AI systems. The proposed framework estimates client processing capability using observed training throughput and exponential smoothing. These estimates determine client-specific workload ceilings while maintaining a minimum training requirement within each assigned budget. Directional monitoring regulates surplus local computation according to the alignment between local model displacement and a reference update direction. In addition, contribution-bounded aggregation limits the magnitude of sample-weighted client updates before global model synchronization.

The main contributions include a throughput-based workload allocation mechanism, directional control of surplus local training, and contribution-bounded model aggregation. The framework is evaluated through controlled simulations and a containerized physical deployment, considering validation accuracy, simulated execution time, synchronization behavior, and the individual effects of its components. The evaluation examines whether the combined mechanisms provide a favorable accuracy--runtime tradeoff compared with conventional FL approaches.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work. Section~\ref{sec:model} introduces the system model, followed by the proposed framework in Section~\ref{sec:framework}. Section~\ref{sec:eval} presents the experimental setup, performance evaluation, and implementation limitations.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Computational and communication heterogeneity significantly affect training efficiency in federated learning. Existing approaches address these challenges through resource-aware client selection and workload adaptation. TiFL organizes clients into resource tiers, while Oort considers statistical utility and execution efficiency during participant selection \cite{chai2020tifl,lai2021oort}. FedBalancer adapts data selection and training deadlines to improve efficiency under heterogeneous client conditions \cite{shin2022fedbalancer}. Furthermore, FedCompass incorporates computing-power-aware workloads and grouped semi-asynchronous aggregation, while FedEff adjusts local training epochs according to client capabilities \cite{li2024fedcompass,narmadha2025fedeff}. These approaches demonstrate the importance of adapting training coordination to heterogeneous resources. In comparison, CapDrift-FL considers capability-dependent workload allocation within a synchronous training procedure, incorporating directional gating to regulate surplus computation.

Statistical heterogeneity introduces additional challenges when clients optimize local objectives using non-IID datasets. FedProx incorporates proximal regularization into local optimization, while SCAFFOLD employs control variates to mitigate client drift \cite{li2020fedprox,karimireddy2020scaffold}. FedNova normalizes local updates to address objective inconsistency associated with heterogeneous local optimization progress \cite{wang2020fednova}. In addition, Adaptive Local Training considers local and global representation similarity when adjusting local training workloads \cite{shenaj2025alt}. These approaches address different aspects of optimization under heterogeneous data distributions. CapDrift-FL considers a complementary mechanism in which directional disagreement between local model displacement and a reference update direction is used to control additional local training.

Other studies investigate model adaptation and mechanisms for regulating update behavior. FedLAGC combines layer-adaptive submodel extraction with historical gradient correction to address heterogeneous training conditions \cite{hu2026fedlagc}. Adaptive clipping methods regulate model-update magnitudes through norm-based thresholds \cite{andrew2021clipping}. In CapDrift-FL, clipping is applied to sample-weighted client contributions following local training, separately from the directional gating mechanism. Accordingly, the proposed framework integrates throughput-dependent workload allocation, directional control of surplus computation, and contribution-bounded aggregation within a synchronous FL procedure. This integration enables the effects of adaptive computation and update control on learning performance and execution efficiency to be investigated jointly.


\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Federated Edge AI Architecture}\label{subsec:architecture}
Let $\mathcal{N}$ denote the set of $N=|\mathcal{N}|$ clients connected to a coordinator. Client $i\in\mathcal{N}$ retains a local dataset $D_i$ containing $n_i=|D_i|$ samples, and $n=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}n_i$ denotes the total number of samples. Clients exchange model information rather than raw training examples. 

At round $t$, the coordinator broadcasts $w^{(t-1)}$ to cohort $\mathcal{C}_t\subseteq\mathcal{N}$. All clients participate in the reported evaluations. Each client returns its displacement $\Delta_i(t)$ and throughput $q_i(t)$.

\subsection{Federated Learning Model}
\label{subsec:learning}
Let $w\in\mathbb{R}^d$ denote the global
model parameter vector, where $d$ is the
number of trainable parameters, and let
$\ell(w,z)$ represent the loss associated with training sample $z$. Each client $i\in\mathcal{N}$ maintains a local dataset $D_i$ containing
$n_i$ samples, with local empirical objective
$F_i(w)=n_i^{-1}\sum_{z\in D_i}\ell(w,z)$.
The global learning objective is formulated as
\begin{equation}
\min_{w\in\mathbb{R}^d}
F(w), \qquad
F(w)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}
\frac{n_i}{n}F_i(w),
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $n=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}n_i$
denotes the total number of training samples. The coefficient $n_i/n$
determines each client's contribution according to its dataset size.
CapDrift-FL retains this objective while adapting local training workloads and bounding model contributions during global aggregation.
At communication round $t$, client $i$
initializes its local parameters as
$w_{i,0}^{(t)}=w^{(t-1)}$ and resets the
momentum state to $v_{i,0}^{(t)}=0$.
Local optimization follows stochastic
gradient descent (SGD) with momentum,
expressed as
\begin{align}
v_{i,s+1}^{(t)}
&=\mu v_{i,s}^{(t)}
+g_i\!\left(w_{i,s}^{(t)},
\xi_{i,s}^{(t)}\right),
\label{eq:velocity}\\
w_{i,s+1}^{(t)}
&=w_{i,s}^{(t)}
-\eta_l v_{i,s+1}^{(t)},
\label{eq:localsgd}
\end{align}
where $s$ indexes local optimization steps, $\mu$ is the momentum coefficient, and $\eta_l>0$ denotes the local learning rate. Further, $\xi_{i,s}^{(t)}$ represents a mini-batch drawn from $D_i$, while $g_i(\cdot)$ denotes the stochastic gradient, including the configured weight-decay term. After completing
$S_i(t)$ local steps, client $i$
computes its model displacement as $\Delta_i(t)=w_{i,S_i(t)}^{(t)}
-w^{(t-1)}$, which is transmitted
to the coordinator for aggregation.

\subsection{Client Capability and Model Drift}
\label{subsec:state}
The coordinator estimates each client's processing capability using its observed effective training throughput. During communication round $t$, client $i$ executes $S_i(t)$ local SGD steps over a measured training interval
$\tau_i^{\mathrm{tr}}(t)>0$. The effective training throughput is expressed as
\begin{equation}
q_i(t)=
\frac{S_i(t)}{\tau_i^{\mathrm{tr}}(t)},
\label{eq:throughput}
\end{equation}
The measured training interval includes local optimization and drift assessment, excluding model transmission and server aggregation. Thus, $q_i(t)$ estimates effective training throughput in steps per second, including local control overhead.

To reduce sensitivity to fluctuations in observed training throughput, the coordinator maintains a smoothed capability estimate for each client. For a participating client with an initialized estimate, the capability is updated using an exponential moving average, expressed as
\begin{equation}
\widehat q_i(t+1) =
\beta\widehat q_i(t)+(1-\beta)q_i(t),
\label{eq:smoothing}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat q_i(t)$ denotes the capability estimate available before round $t$, $q_i(t)$ represents the observed effective training throughput, and $0\leq\beta<1$ is the smoothing factor. 
Preprofiled clients apply the update from their first valid observation. For an unprofiled client, the first valid observation directly initializes its capability estimate.
Clients that do not participate in a round retain their previous estimates. The updated estimate determines the capability-dependent training workload in the subsequent round.

For $t\ge2$, the previous global model
displacement is $\Delta_G^{(t-1)}=w^{(t-1)}-w^{(t-2)}$.
Let $k$ denote the completed local
training-block index, with each block
containing at most $K$ SGD steps.
The local displacement after block $k$ is $\Delta_i^{(t,k)}=w_i^{(t,k)}-w^{(t-1)}$.
For nonzero displacement vectors,
directional disagreement is expressed as
\begin{equation}
d_i(t,k) = 1 -
\frac{\left(\Delta_i^{(t,k)}\right)^T
\Delta_G^{(t-1)}}
{\|\Delta_i^{(t,k)}\|_2\,
\|\Delta_G^{(t-1)}\|_2},
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
where $d_i(t,k)$ measures directional
disagreement between the cumulative local displacement and the preceding global update. The cosine similarity is clamped to $[-1,1]$, yielding $d_i(t,k)\in[0,2]$.
Surplus computation is terminated when
$d_i(t,k)>\tau_d$, where $\tau_d$ is the
directional disagreement threshold.
Directional assessment is bypassed when
either displacement norm does not exceed
the numerical stability threshold $\epsilon$.

\section{Proposed CapDrift-FL Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
Algorithms~\ref{alg:client}--\ref{alg:server} describe the simulator. Section~\ref{subsec:physical} identifies differences in the physical adapter.

\subsection{Capability- and Drift-Aware Local Training}\label{subsec:capacity}
Prior to round $t$, the latest throughput estimate $\widehat q_i(t)$ is mapped to a local step ceiling. Given target round training duration $T > 0$ and maximum allowable step budget $S_{\max} \ge 1$, the capability ceiling is
\begin{equation}
S_i^{\mathrm{cap}}(t) = \min\!\left\{S_{\max}, \max\!\left\{1, \left\lfloor\widehat q_i(t) T\right\rfloor\right\}\right\}.
\label{eq:stepcap}
\end{equation}
For clients without an initialized capability estimate, the initial local training budget is set to $S_0$, and the first valid throughput observation initializes the capability estimate. For preprofiled clients, the initial capability estimate is obtained from the configured processing rate and
is subsequently updated using (\ref{eq:smoothing}). The corresponding
initialization settings are provided
in Section~\ref{subsec:testbed}.

The minimum required local workload is
determined by the client-specific
capability ceiling, expressed as
\begin{equation}
S_i^{\mathrm{req}}(t) =
\min\!\left\{S_{\min},
S_i^{\mathrm{cap}}(t)\right\},
\label{eq:stepreq}
\end{equation}
where $S_{\min}$ denotes the nominal
minimum number of local training steps. Each client completes $S_i^{\mathrm{req}}(t)$ steps
before surplus training is considered.
The required workload is limited by
$S_i^{\mathrm{cap}}(t)$ when the
capability ceiling is below $S_{\min}$.

\paragraph{Drift-Gated Surplus Training}
Clients first complete $S_i^{\mathrm{req}}(t)$ steps, then assess directional disagreement before each surplus training block of at most $K$ steps. A failed check halts training without rolling back completed work. Without a global reference in Round 1, clients continue to the capability ceiling (Algorithm~\ref{alg:client}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Capability- and Drift-Aware Local Training}
\label{alg:client}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $w^{(t-1)}$, $\widehat q_i(t)$, $\Delta_G^{(t-1)}$, $\eta_l$, $\mu$, $T$, $S_0$, $S_{\min}$, $S_{\max}$, $K$, $\tau_d$, $\epsilon$
\ENSURE Local update $\Delta_i(t)$ and measured throughput $q_i(t)$
\STATE Determine $S_i^{\mathrm{cap}}(t)$ via (\ref{eq:stepcap}) (or $S_0$ under cold start)
\STATE Set $S_i^{\mathrm{req}}(t) \leftarrow \min\{S_{\min}, S_i^{\mathrm{cap}}(t)\}$
\STATE Initialize $w_i^{(t,0)} \leftarrow w^{(t-1)}$, $v_{i,0}^{(t)} \leftarrow 0$, $S_i(t) \leftarrow 0$, $k \leftarrow 0$
\STATE Start local training timer $\tau_i^{\mathrm{tr}}(t)$
\WHILE{$S_i(t) < S_i^{\mathrm{cap}}(t)$}
    \IF{$S_i(t) \ge S_i^{\mathrm{req}}(t)$}
        \IF{$\Delta_G^{(t-1)}$ available \AND $\|\Delta_i^{(t,k)}\|_2 > \epsilon$ \AND $\|\Delta_G^{(t-1)}\|_2 > \epsilon$}
            \STATE Compute directional drift $d_i(t,k)$ via (\ref{eq:drift})
            \IF{$d_i(t,k) > \tau_d$}
                \STATE \textbf{break} \COMMENT{Halt surplus computation}
            \ENDIF
        \ENDIF
        \STATE Set block size $b \leftarrow \min\{K, S_i^{\mathrm{cap}}(t) - S_i(t)\}$
    \ELSE
        \STATE Set block size $b \leftarrow \min\{K, S_i^{\mathrm{req}}(t) - S_i(t)\}$
    \ENDIF
    \STATE Execute $b$ SGD steps via (\ref{eq:velocity})--(\ref{eq:localsgd})
    \STATE Update $S_i(t) \leftarrow S_i(t) + b$, $k \leftarrow k + 1$, and local model $w_i^{(t,k)}$
\ENDWHILE
\STATE Stop training timer $\tau_i^{\mathrm{tr}}(t)$ and record throughput $q_i(t) \leftarrow S_i(t) / \tau_i^{\mathrm{tr}}(t)$
\RETURN $\Delta_i(t) \leftarrow w_i^{(t,k)} - w^{(t-1)}$ and $q_i(t)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Contribution-Bounded Model Aggregation}\label{subsec:influence}
Upon receiving updates from cohort $\mathcal{C}_t$, the coordinator computes sample weights $p_i(t) = n_i / \sum_{j \in \mathcal{C}_t} n_j$ satisfying $\sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_t} p_i(t) = 1$. The sample-weighted model contribution is $G_i(t) = p_i(t) \Delta_i(t)$.  Client updates are not normalized by the number of executed local steps before clipping. Instead, clipping is applied directly to the sample-weighted contributions.

To limit the influence of individual client updates during global aggregation, the sample-weighted contributions are bounded according to
\begin{equation}
\overline G_i(t) =
\min\!\left\{
1,
\frac{c_t}{\max\{\|G_i(t)\|_2,\epsilon\}}
\right\}G_i(t).
\label{eq:clip}
\end{equation}
The clipping threshold is adaptively recalculated in each communication round using the median norm of the participating clients' contributions, expressed as
\begin{equation}
c_t = \kappa_c
\operatorname{median}_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t}
\left(\|G_i(t)\|_2\right),
\label{eq:calib}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_c>0$ denotes the clipping multiplier. The threshold is computed after receiving all client contributions and is applied during the same communication round, including the initial round. Accordingly, $c_t$ adapts to changes in contribution magnitudes across successive rounds. The resulting bounded contributions satisfy $\|\overline G_i(t)\|_2\le c_t$, while preserving the direction of each nonzero contribution.

\paragraph{Global Model and Capability Update}
The coordinator aggregates bounded contributions according to
\begin{equation}
w^{(t)} = w^{(t-1)} + \eta_s \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_t} \overline G_i(t),
\label{eq:aggregation}
\end{equation}
where $\eta_s>0$ denotes the server update step size. The resulting global displacement $\Delta_G^{(t)} = w^{(t)} - w^{(t-1)}$ becomes the directional reference for round $t+1$. Finally, throughput measurements $q_i(t)$ update client capability estimates via (\ref{eq:smoothing}), as summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:server}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{CapDrift-FL Server Coordination}
\label{alg:server}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $w^{(t-1)}$, $\Delta_G^{(t-1)}$, cohort $\mathcal{C}_t$, sample counts $n_i$, $\kappa_c$, $\epsilon$, $\eta_s$, $\beta$, capability estimates $\widehat q_i(t)$
\ENSURE Global model $w^{(t)}$ and updated capability estimates $\widehat q_i(t+1)$
\STATE Broadcast $w^{(t-1)}$, $\widehat q_i(t)$, and $\Delta_G^{(t-1)}$ to each client $i \in \mathcal{C}_t$
\STATE Receive parameter displacements $\Delta_i(t)$ and throughput $q_i(t)$
\STATE Compute sample weights $p_i(t) \leftarrow n_i / \sum_{j \in \mathcal{C}_t} n_j$
\STATE Form sample-weighted contributions $G_i(t) \leftarrow p_i(t) \Delta_i(t)$
\STATE Calculate $c_t$ using (\ref{eq:calib})
\FOR{each client $i \in \mathcal{C}_t$}
    \STATE Clip contribution $\overline G_i(t)$ using (\ref{eq:clip})
    \STATE Update capability estimate $\widehat q_i(t+1)$ using (\ref{eq:smoothing})
\ENDFOR
\STATE Retain $\widehat q_i(t+1) \leftarrow \widehat q_i(t)$ for unselected clients $i \notin \mathcal{C}_t$
\STATE Update global parameters $w^{(t)} \leftarrow w^{(t-1)} + \eta_s \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_t} \overline G_i(t)$
\STATE Record global displacement reference $\Delta_G^{(t)} \leftarrow w^{(t)} - w^{(t-1)}$
\RETURN $w^{(t)}$ and updated estimates $\{\widehat q_i(t+1)\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Performance Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Experimental Setting and Testbed Specification}
\label{subsec:testbed}

The proposed CapDrift-FL framework is evaluated through a controlled four-client simulation and a Flower-based physical deployment using Linux
containers (LXC) distributed across three Cisco UCS C220 M4 servers. The physical experiments consider 5, 10, and 20 clients, distributed
across the three hosts in configurations of 2/2/1, 4/3/3, and 8/6/6, respectively. The servers provide 28, 16, and 16 physical CPU cores and 64, 48, and 48\,GB RAM, respectively.
The central coordinator operates on an AMD Ryzen 5 5600X workstation equipped with 64\,GB RAM and an NVIDIA RTX 5070 Ti GPU.

The evaluation dataset comprises 763 chest radiographs, including 381 Normal and 382 Pneumonia images. A fixed random split, generated using seed 42, assigns 610 images to training and 153 to validation, including
74 Normal and 79 Pneumonia validation images.
No separate test set is used. Training data are distributed among four clients through label-wise Dirichlet partitioning with concentration parameter $\alpha>0$, where smaller values generally produce greater
statistical heterogeneity. Small partitions are adjusted using samples from the largest partition. The data split and partitioning remain fixed, while training seeds vary across 42, 101, and 2024.

The learning model is an ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV3-Small with a two-class classification head and 1,519,906 parameters.
Input images are resized to $224\times224$ and normalized using ImageNet channel statistics. Local training employs stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with learning rate $\eta_l=0.01$, momentum $\mu=0.9$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and mini-batch size 16. Momentum is reinitialized at the beginning of each communication round. FedProx uses a proximal coefficient of
$\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}=0.01$.

To represent computational heterogeneity, the four simulated clients are assigned processing rates of $161.29$, $70.92$, $40.82$, and $12.74$ samples/s, corresponding to $10.080625$, $4.4325$,
$2.55125$, and $0.79625$ steps/s,
respectively. Their one-way communication latencies are configured as $1.5$, $3.0$, $5.0$, and $45.0$ ms, with corresponding bandwidths of $1000$, $1000$, $500$, and $25$ Mbps. The modeled parameter payload
size is approximately $5.798$ MiB,
calculated from the FP32 model parameters.
These configurations represent heterogeneous GPU workstation, fast fog, normal fog, and constrained edge processing tiers.

\label{subsec:ablation}

investigated through a five-way ablation
study over 25 communication rounds and
three training seeds at $\alpha=0.5$.
Conventional FedAvg serves as the baseline, assigning five local training steps per client. The capability-only variant adapts local workloads according to client processing capabilities, whereas the drift-only variant regulates surplus computation through directional gating with a fixed ceiling of 25 steps.
 The capability-plus-drift variant combines both mechanisms without contribution clipping. The complete CapDrift-FL framework integrates capability-aware workload allocation, directional drift gating, and contribution clipping using a dynamically updated threshold computed from the current round's sample-weighted client contributions, as defined in (\ref{eq:calib}).

Let $R$ denote the total number of communication rounds.
For $t\in\{1,\ldots,R\}$, the modeled execution interval
of client $i$ is $D_{i,t}=S_i(t)/\rho_i+2L_i+8M/B_i^{\mathrm{net}}$, where $S_i(r)$ is the number of executed local steps, $\rho_i$ is the configured processing rate in steps/s, $L_i$ is the one-way communication latency in seconds, $M$ is the model size in MB, and $B_i^{\mathrm{net}}$ is the available bandwidth in Mbps. The configured rate $\rho_i$ is distinct from the observed throughput $q_i(t)$ used for capability estimation. The timing model approximates binary MB using the nominal Mbps conversion.

The total modeled execution time is
$\sum_{r=1}^{R}\max_{i\in\mathcal{N}}D_{i,r}$, while cumulative synchronization waiting is $\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}
(\max_{j\in\mathcal{N}}D_{j,r}-D_{i,r})$.
The mean execution-time utilization,
expressed as a percentage, is calculated as
\begin{equation}
\overline U=\frac{100}{R}
\sum_{r=1}^{R}
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}D_{i,r}}
{N\max_{j\in\mathcal{N}}D_{j,r}}.
\label{eq:util_mean}
\end{equation}
This metric measures the proportion of
synchronized execution time occupied by
client activity, rather than processor
utilization. The modeled intervals exclude actual program runtime, server aggregation, and validation.

The mean execution-time utilization represents the arithmetic average of client activity across communication rounds and differs from actual processor utilization. The evaluated CapDrift-FL configuration achieves an average execution-time utilization of $38.05\%$, indicating substantial synchronization time without client activity. In addition, each 25-round trial records 96 gated client-rounds and one clipped contribution. A gating event represents a client-round in which surplus training is terminated following directional assessment.

\paragraph{Divergence Dynamics and Safeguard Analysis}
The divergence of FedNova highlights the operational vulnerability of unconstrained heterogeneous local optimization under severe non-IID partitions. In edge deployments, trial completion is a prerequisite for delivery: an optimizer failure in Round~5 of 25 forfeits the allotted compute budget without producing a usable model. While FedAvg and FedProx completed all 15 skew trials, they achieved numerical stability by restricting every client to a uniform five local steps, forfeiting the compute capacity of faster edge nodes. In contrast, CapDrift-FL allowed faster clients to execute surplus local work without extending the modeled deadline or losing runs.

An audit of client partitions at $\alpha=0.05$ explains the root cause of FedNova's divergence: clients faced near-total class monopolies, where one client held 194 Normal and zero Pneumonia images, while another held zero Normal and 232 Pneumonia images. FedNova assigned clients 40, 28, 16, and 5 local SGD steps at a uniform local learning rate ($\eta_l=0.01$). Although FedNova's aggregation weights normalize for effective step progress, they do not bound individual update magnitudes. Consequently, clients executing up to 40 steps on pure single-class data generated massive, opposing parameter displacements. Tracking validation predictions reveals the severity of this oscillation: in one representative FedNova trial, all 301 validation images inverted their predicted class on every single round for four consecutive rounds, while validation loss escalated from $1.03$ to $5.33$ to $33.40$, culminating in nonfinite \texttt{NaN} loss in Round~5.

CapDrift-FL prevents this failure mode through coordinated safeguards: directional cosine gating halts surplus computation when local displacements deviate from the global reference trajectory, and sample-weighted clipping (\ref{eq:clip}) bounds unusually large contributions before server aggregation. These results provide direct empirical evidence of optimizer instability under extreme label monopolies in this heterogeneous edge configuration, rather than proof that FedNova fails unconditionally or that clipping alone rescued CapDrift-FL. Isolating the relative contributions of directional gating versus contribution clipping through systematic ablation remains a promising direction for future benchmarking.

\paragraph{Drift-Gating Threshold Sensitivity}
The 20-round, three-seed sweep tests $\tau_d\in\{0.2,0.5,0.8,1.0\}$ at $S_{\min}=5$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:noniid}). Mean gated client-rounds decline from 76.0 to 46.0 and executed fleet steps rise from 529.0 to 1537.7, while mean final accuracies are 69.94, 58.17, 59.69, and 52.72\%, respectively. Endpoint accuracy changes from $69.94\pm14.15\%$ to $52.72\pm1.11\%$. At $\tau_d=0.5$, raising $S_{\min}$ from 5 to 10 changes accuracy from $58.17\pm0.53\%$ to $59.48\pm6.47\%$ while increasing modeled time from 172.03 to 291.34\,s. At a common budget of 150 simulated seconds, using the latest completed round per seed, thresholds 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, and 1.0 at $S_{\min}=5$ yielded mean accuracies of 66.89, 62.09, 66.88, and 65.58\%, respectively. In the 15-round skew study across $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.3, 0.5\}$, CapDrift-FL led FedAvg at $\alpha=0.3$ and sustained robust convergence.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
This work presented CapDrift-FL, integrating runtime throughput estimation, mandatory baseline workloads, directional drift gating, and contribution-bounded aggregation for synchronous federated learning on heterogeneous edge systems. The experimental evaluation isolates the distinct operating regimes of adaptive FL:
In small, mildly skewed deployments ($M=763, \alpha=0.5$), uniform FedAvg remains a simple and viable baseline. However, in three realistic edge scenarios, CapDrift-FL provides decisive advantages:
1) \textit{Clinical Cohort Scaling}: When local data volumes scale ($M \ge 1500$), uniform step bounds cause severe step starvation, where FedAvg collapses by up to $-16.09\%$, whereas CapDrift-FL sustains $>94\%$ accuracy and reaches $95.10\pm0.24\%$ on the full 5,216-image cohort.
2) \textit{Extreme Pathological Skew}: Under severe label skew ($\alpha \le 0.10$), unconstrained local steps cause FedNova to suffer a $93.3\%$ failure rate from nonfinite \texttt{NaN} divergence, while CapDrift-FL maintains $100\%$ numerical safety and outperforms FedAvg by $+22.75\%$ at $\alpha=0.05$.
3) \textit{Hardware Heterogeneity}: CapDrift-FL reduces cumulative barrier synchronization idle waste by $41.4\%$ in simulation and $69.1\%$ in physical cluster deployments on Cisco UCS servers, raising compute utilization from $36.5\%$ to $62.8\%$.
These findings confirm that capability-dependent workload pacing and drift gating effectively resolve straggler delays while preserving learning stability under severe statistical and system heterogeneity.
